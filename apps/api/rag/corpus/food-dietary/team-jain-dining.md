# Eating Jain in Japan (team-written tips)

These are our own suggestions, not official guidance. They follow the same rules as the Mitra Japan menu scanner.

## Vegetarian is not Jain

- A dish can be vegetarian and still contain **onion, garlic, potato, carrot, radish (daikon), ginger** or other root vegetables. Our scanner flags all of these as a conflict for Jain diets.
- **Mushrooms** are treated as a conflict for Jain diets in our scanner, because many Jains avoid them.
- **Egg** and **honey** are conflicts.
- **Dashi** is usually fish stock. Even kombu dashi gets "ask staff" for Jain diets in our scanner.

## Words to watch for on menus

- 玉ねぎ / たまねぎ (onion), ねぎ / ネギ (spring onion), にんにく (garlic)
- じゃがいも (potato), 人参 / にんじん (carrot), 大根 (daikon), 生姜 / しょうが (ginger)
- きのこ / しいたけ (mushrooms), 卵 / たまご (egg), だし / 出汁 (stock)

## Practical habits

- Show the **Jain staff card** in the scanner. It explains no meat, fish, egg, onion, garlic or root vegetables in Japanese.
- Ask for dishes to be made without onion or garlic rather than picking them out.
- Temple cuisine (shojin ryori) traditionally avoids garlic and onion (JNTO vegetarian guide), but it can still use root vegetables. Ask when you book.
- Tell hotels and ryokan about Jain needs **when you book**, not on arrival.
- Plain rice, fruit and some packaged snacks are easier fallbacks. Still read the label with the scanner.

## Where to check
