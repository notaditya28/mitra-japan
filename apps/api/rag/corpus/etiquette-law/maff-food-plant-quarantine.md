# Bringing food, fruit, plants or meat into Japan

This matters for Indian travellers who pack home food, fruit or snacks.

## Not allowed or restricted

- **Most fresh fruit and vegetables are prohibited.** Examples on the page include mangoes, citrus, pears and apples.
- Seedlings, seeds, grains and beans need a **phytosanitary certificate** from the exporting country, even when they are not prohibited.
- **Soil** and certain live insects are prohibited.
- **Meat and meat products** are generally prohibited, even vacuum-packed or bought duty-free. Examples include ham, sausages, bacon, jerky, meat buns and dumplings.
- **In-flight meals** with fresh produce or meat cannot be taken off the plane, so eat them on board.

## At arrival

- Quarantine inspectors at airports, seaports and post offices may ask about prohibited items, and detector dogs may be used. You may be asked to show your baggage.
- If you have touched livestock or visited a farm before arriving, go to the Animal Quarantine counter to have your shoes and clothing disinfected.

## Penalties

Illegally bringing these items in can mean **up to three years' imprisonment or a fine of up to 3 million yen**; up to 50 million yen for businesses. Police are notified when it appears deliberate.

## By mail

You cannot send these items to Japan by international mail. Ask family not to post them to you while you're in Japan.

## What this means for packed Indian food

Whether a particular packaged or cooked food is allowed depends on what it contains. **Check before you pack**; this summary does not cover every product. The Embassy of Japan in India also warns: do not bring meat products, vegetables or fruits into Japan.
