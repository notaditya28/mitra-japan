# Types of accommodation in Japan

## Western-style hotels

Found in every major city. Large chains are common and staff can usually speak English.

## Business hotels

Economical and no-frills, usually near train stations. Rooms are smaller with fewer amenities.

## Ryokan (Japanese inns)

- Found across Japan, especially in onsen (hot spring) resorts.
- Rooms are usually one tatami space; staff lay out futon bedding on the floor.
- Rates are usually **per person and include two meals, dinner and breakfast**. Most serve kaiseki cuisine.
- Our suggestion, not from JNTO: because meals are included, tell the ryokan about **vegetarian, Jain or halal needs when booking**.

## Minshuku

Usually family-owned guest houses in resort or tourist areas outside big cities. Cheaper than ryokan, with fewer amenities.

## Shukubo (temple stays)

You can join Buddhist practices such as meditation and sutra-copying, and try **shojin ryori**, the vegetarian cuisine eaten by monks.

## Youth hostels

More than 200 across Japan, simple and low-cost, with single or dormitory rooms. Meals are not included, and there are more rules, such as curfews.

## Booking

Places sell out quickly in peak seasons in popular cities, so book well in advance.
