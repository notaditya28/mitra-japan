# Calling an ambulance in Japan (119)

## The number

- **Call 119** for an ambulance. The ambulance service is available to anyone in Japan.
- You can call 119 from a mobile phone, a fixed line or a public telephone.
- The 119 dispatcher first asks whether it is a **fire or a medical emergency**, so 119 is also the number for fire.

## What to say, in order

1. **"Medical emergency."** Say this first.
2. **Where you are.** Give the address. If you don't know it, describe a nearby building or intersection as a landmark.
3. **What happened.** Describe the symptoms and when they started.
4. **Age.** Give the patient's age; if you are calling for someone else, say how old they are or look.
5. **Your name and phone number**, so the crew can reach you after the call if they can't find you.

If the situation is serious, the ambulance is sent before all the questions have been asked. Stay calm and speak slowly. If people nearby speak Japanese, ask them to help with the call.

## While you wait

- If others are with you, send someone to the place where the ambulance will arrive to guide the crew.
- If you can, prepare:
  - your passport
  - cash or a credit card to pay at the hospital
  - your current medication
  - a short note of the most painful symptoms, past illnesses and regular medication
- When the ambulance arrives, tell the crew how the accident or illness happened and what changed before they arrived.

## Heat illness

- Summer in Japan is hot and humid, sometimes above 35°C. The pamphlet says more than 40,000 people a year are taken to hospital with heat illness.
- **Prevention:** drink water even when you're not thirsty, keep rooms below about 28°C with air-conditioning or a fan, wear loose cool clothing and shade yourself outside, and rest.
- **Call an ambulance without hesitation** if someone can't drink or move because of strong weakness, or is unconscious or having convulsions.
- **First aid while waiting:** move the person somewhere cool, loosen their clothing, lay them down, cool the armpits and the base of the thighs, and give small sips of water if they can drink.
