# IC cards, paper tickets and buses

## IC cards

- Prepaid, rechargeable cards that work on most transport in most cities. **Suica** and **PASMO** are common around Tokyo (Kanto); **ICOCA** and **TOICA** are used in other areas.
- **Buying:** from a ticket machine or counter at most stations. Most cards have a **500-yen deposit**, refunded at a ticket counter when you return the card.
- **Welcome Suica:** a deposit-free card for tourists, sold at certain Kanto locations and valid for up to 28 days.
- **Topping up:** at ticket machines in almost every station.
- **Phone:** Mobile Suica or PASMO can go in a smartphone wallet, but this may not work on every phone.
- **Gates:** tap to enter and tap again to exit. If the gate stays shut, the reader may have missed the card or the balance is too low.
- **Not accepted everywhere:** highway buses, for example, don't take IC cards. Look for the IC logo.
- IC cards also pay at some vending machines, shops and station lockers.

## Paper tickets

- Buy from station machines; the fare chart shows the price to your destination.
- Insert the ticket at the entry gate and keep it. You need it to exit.
- Paid the wrong fare? Use the fare adjustment machine inside the gate, or a staffed gate.

## Buses

- **Numbered-ticket buses:** board at the rear and take a numbered ticket. The screen at the front shows the fare for your number; pay at the front when you get off. Overpayment isn't refunded, so pay the exact amount; most buses have change machines.
- **Flat-fare buses:** board at the front and pay as you get on.
- With an IC card on a bus that accepts it, tap when you board and again when you get off.
