# Halal food and prayer for Muslim travellers

## Halal food

- Japan has **no central halal accreditation body**, so checking certification takes effort.
- Halal-certified products are mostly frozen meat and spices, not ready meals. Ordinary supermarkets usually don't stock them; some ethnic grocery shops sell halal curry meals.

## Hidden alcohol and animal ingredients

- Japanese cooking often uses **sake** and **mirin** (a sweet cooking wine).
- Some miso contains alcohol unless it is additive-free and labelled *mu-ten-ka* (無添加).
- Gelatin and animal fats can turn up in bread, snacks and sweets.

## Restaurants

- Some places call themselves halal-friendly but **don't have a separate halal-only kitchen**.
- Alcohol is served in most Japanese restaurants.
- Resources JNTO lists: Japan Welcome Guide for Muslim Travelers, Japan Guide for Muslim Visitors, Halal Gourmet Japan, Halal Media Japan, HalalJapan.jp.

## Prayer

- Prayer rooms and mosques are mostly in larger cities.
- Major airports, tourist areas and Muslim-friendly hotels increasingly offer prayer spaces. Some hotels provide prayer mats and Qibla-facing spaces.
- Rentals with a kitchenette let you prepare your own halal meals.
