# JMA Emergency Warnings and alert levels

## What an Emergency Warning is

The Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) issues **Emergency Warnings** when there is a high chance of catastrophe from a natural event of unusually large scale. They have been in use since 30 August 2013. When one is issued, people should **take every possible measure to protect themselves**.

## What they cover

- Heavy rain, flood, landslide and storm surge, at **Alert Level 5**.
- Storm, snowstorm, heavy snow and high waves.
- **Tsunami**, issued as Major Tsunami Warnings.
- **Volcanic activity**, issued as Volcanic Warnings at Level 4 or higher.
- **Earthquakes**, issued as Earthquake Early Warnings forecasting shaking of seismic intensity 6-lower or more.

## Alert levels for rain, floods, landslides and storm surge

- **Level 3 (Warning):** elderly people, people with disabilities and others who need more time should evacuate.
- **Level 4 (Urgent Warning):** **everyone** should evacuate from dangerous areas.
- **Level 5 (Emergency Warning):** disaster may already be happening. Finish evacuating **before** Level 4 is reached; don't wait for Level 5.

## What to do

- **Act immediately** to protect your life, even if the area hasn't had a disaster for decades.
- **Tsunami:** go at once to high ground or a designated evacuation building.
- **Volcano:** evacuate, or prepare to evacuate, from alert areas.
- **Earthquake:** stay calm and protect yourself based on your surroundings.
- **Rain, flood or landslide:** follow local evacuation advice.
- Areas without an Emergency Warning should not let their guard down.
