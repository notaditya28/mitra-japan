# Japanese manners and etiquette

## Shoes and slippers

- Take off your shoes at the entrance, put on the indoor slippers provided, and leave your shoes in the shoebox or to one side.
- Take slippers off on tatami mats. Use the separate toilet slippers in the restroom.

## Eating

- Say "itadakimasu" before eating and "gochisosama-deshita" afterwards.
- Leaving food is frowned upon, and asking to take leftovers home is generally not accepted. Order what you can finish.
- In a group, wait until everyone is served, and ask before taking the last piece of a shared dish.
- You can eat sushi with chopsticks or your hands. Don't dip the rice in soy sauce or mix wasabi into it.
- Slurping noodles is normal but not required.
- If you'd prefer another utensil, ask; traditional restaurants may not have one.

## On trains and buses

- Speak softly, keep your phone on silent and avoid phone calls.
- When boarding, line up to the side so passengers can get off first.
- Give priority seats to elderly, pregnant or disabled passengers.

## Smoking

- **Smoking while walking is prohibited and can be fined.** Smoke only in designated areas.
- Dropping cigarette butts on the ground is prohibited.

## Bowing and tipping

- Visitors are more often bowed to than expected to bow. A small bow with "thank you" is a nice gesture.
- Tipping is not expected at Western-style hotels.
