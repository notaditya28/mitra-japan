# How Japanese visas work (MOFA overview)

## Visa versus landing permission

- A visa is one of the requirements for entering Japan, but it does **not guarantee entry**. An immigration officer at the port of entry grants "landing permission" when the requirements are met.
- Visas are issued by Japan's embassies, consulates general and consular offices abroad. They **cannot be obtained on arrival or while in Japan**.
- People often say "visa" when they mean "status of residence"; questions about status of residence go to the Immigration Services Agency of Japan.

## Short-term stay

- Short-term stay means up to 90 days for tourism, business, visiting friends or relatives and similar purposes, **without paid work**.
- Nationals who are not visa-exempt need a visa for a short-term stay. The MOFA page links a separate list of visa-exempt countries and regions.

## Application steps (general)

1. Prepare all the required documents. If someone in Japan is inviting or guaranteeing you, they send their documents to you, not to the ministry or embassy.
2. Requirements vary by country and purpose, so check the website of the Japanese mission where you will apply.
3. Apply at the consular section of the mission covering your place of residence, at an accredited agency, at a Japan Visa Application Centre, or online, using the method that mission specifies.
4. Processing takes about one week once all requirements are met. Extra documents may be requested, and some cases take longer.
5. The mission or agency tells you the result.

## Other notes from the page

- MOFA strongly recommends international travel medical insurance for trips to Japan.
- Standard forms (visa application form, letter of guarantee, invitation letter, itinerary in Japan) are downloadable from the page. A company or personal seal on the letter of guarantee and invitation letter is no longer required.
- Work, or stays longer than 90 days, generally need a Certificate of Eligibility from the Immigration Services Agency before applying.
- Beware of fraudulent websites, social media and emails that try to take payments from visa applicants.
