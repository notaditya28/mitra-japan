# Japanese deities with Indian origins

Several deities in Japanese Buddhism **developed from** Indian deities. Today they are distinct Japanese deities with their own histories. They are associated with their Indian origins, not "the same as" them.

## Benzaiten (Saraswati)

- Developed from the Indian river goddess Saraswati.
- Associated with music, eloquence and water, and often shown playing a biwa lute.
- Site in our data: Enoshima, Kanagawa.
- Reference: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzaiten

## Kangiten (Ganesha)

- Developed from Ganesha (Vinayaka) through esoteric Buddhism.
- Often shown as a pair of embracing elephant-headed figures, and associated with removing obstacles.
- Site in our data: Matsuchiyama Shoden, Tokyo.
- Reference: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangiten

## Kichijoten (Lakshmi)

- Developed from Lakshmi (Shri), and associated with good fortune, beauty and prosperity.
- Site in our data: Joruri-ji, Kyoto.
- Reference: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kichij%C5%8Dten

## Daikokuten (Mahakala)

- Developed from Mahakala, a fierce form associated with Shiva.
- Later blended with the Shinto god Ōkuninushi into a smiling god of the harvest and kitchens.
- Site in our data: Enryaku-ji, Mount Hiei.
- Reference: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daikokuten

## Bishamonten (Vaishravana / Kubera)

- Developed from Vaishravana, guardian of the north, who is associated with Kubera. In Japan he became an armoured protector.
- Site in our data: Kurama-dera, Kyoto.
- Reference: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishamonten
